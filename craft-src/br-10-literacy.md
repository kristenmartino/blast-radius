# An AI literacy program for 3,000 people

You are Head of AI Transformation at Pellbrook Utilities (fictional), {{b10.staff}} people. The CEO has promised the board that "everyone will be AI-ready by year end." HR has a proposal; the CFO wants the full cost; the union representative has asked what this means for jobs. Companies and prices are invented.

## Packet

### Who works here

| Group | People |
|---|---|
| Executives | {{b10.exec}} |
| People managers | {{b10.mgr}} |
| Office and knowledge staff | {{b10.know}} |
| Field and frontline staff | {{b10.front}} |

### HR's proposal

> {{$b10.delivery}} for an e-learning licence and two trainers. Everyone completes a one-hour AI basics course; managers and office staff add a four-hour course on the assistant; frontline staff get two hours on the tools in their jobs; {{b10.champs}} champions get twelve hours on top of their own group's training; executives get a three-hour session. Success: 95% completion by year end.

### Note from the CFO

> HR says {{$b10.delivery}}. Is that the cost?

### Note from the union representative

> Members want to know whether this training is the first step to cutting jobs.

### Task

Hand back four stages.

1. Set out the audiences and what each should be able to do afterwards.
2. Cost the program in full: learning hours by tier and their cost at {{$b10.rate}} an hour, plus delivery.
3. Replace the success measure with ones that show the program worked.
4. Write the message to staff about job impact, under 120 words.

Planted: HR's figure leaves out staff time, completion is the only success measure, and nobody has answered the job question.

## Key

### Hours and cost

- Basics: {{b10.staff}} × 1 hour = {{b10.hBasics}} hours.
- Role-based: ({{b10.mgr}} + {{b10.know}}) × 4 = {{b10.hRole}} hours.
- Frontline: {{b10.front}} × 2 = {{b10.hFront}} hours.
- Champions: {{b10.champs}} × 12 = {{b10.hChamp}} hours. Executives: {{b10.exec}} × 3 = {{b10.hExec}} hours.
- Total {{b10.hours}} hours × {{$b10.rate}} = {{$b10.timeCost}} of staff time; with {{$b10.delivery}} delivery, {{$b10.total}} in all, {{$b10.perHead}} a person. Staff time is {{b10.timeSharePct}}% of the cost.

### Measures

- Completion shows attendance, not ability. Better: weekly use of the assistant by group; tasks done with it in each role; a short practical check; manager-reported changes in work; incidents and policy questions.

## Model answer

### Stage 1

- Executives: decide where AI fits the strategy and how to judge AI business cases.
- Managers: redesign team work and coach use; answer job questions honestly.
- Office staff: use the assistant on their own tasks safely, and know what not to paste in.
- Frontline: use the tools in their jobs; know where to ask.
- Champions: first-line help in each team.

### Stage 2

- {{b10.hours}} learning hours: {{$b10.timeCost}} of staff time plus {{$b10.delivery}} delivery = {{$b10.total}} ({{$b10.perHead}} a person). HR's figure is the smaller part.

### Stage 3

- Weekly use by group, tasks done with AI per role, a practical check, manager-reported changes, and incidents. Completion stays as an input.

### Stage 4

- "This training is for the jobs you do now: using AI well and safely in your own work. I cannot promise that no job will ever change. I can promise how we will handle it: if AI changes how a role is done, the people affected hear first, we consult the union before any decision, and we offer retraining. If you have a question about your role, ask your manager or me directly."

## Grading notes

- A strong hand-back costs staff time as well as delivery and matches the key's {{$b10.total}}.
- Outcomes per audience are things people can do, not topics covered.
- The job message makes only promises the company can keep, and names the consultation route.
